About this tool

Free online Cron to Text translator — turn complex Cron schedule expressions into clear, human-readable natural language descriptions. Supports standard 5-field format and Linux special strings like @daily, @hourly, @reboot, plus 6-field (with seconds) and 7-field (with seconds and year) extended formats. Enter a Cron expression and get an instant plain-English description.

Perfect for backend developers, DevOps engineers, system administrators, and SREs who need to quickly understand or verify Cron expressions. All processing happens in your browser — no data leaves your device.

Key Features

  • Supports standard 5-field Cron format
  • Supports 6-field (with seconds) and 7-field (with seconds and year) extended formats
  • Supports Linux special strings: @yearly, @monthly, @weekly, @daily, @hourly, @reboot
  • Supports all Cron operators: *, /, -, ,, L, W, #, ?
  • Real-time translation as you type
  • One-click copy results
  • Quick-fill example expressions
  • 100% client-side processing

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the 5 Cron fields?

Minute(0-59), Hour(0-23), Day(1-31), Month(1-12), Weekday(0-7, both 0 and 7 represent Sunday).

Does it support @reboot?

Yes. @reboot means the task runs once at system startup.

What is the difference between 6-field and 7-field formats?

5 fields: Minute Hour Day Month Weekday; 6 fields: Second Minute Hour Day Month Weekday; 7 fields: Second Minute Hour Day Month Weekday Year.

What do L and W mean?

L means Last. L in day field = last day of month, L in weekday field = Saturday. W means nearest weekday, e.g. 15W = nearest weekday to the 15th.

How does the # operator work?

# is used in the weekday field. For example, 3#2 means the second Wednesday of the month.

Is my data safe?

Completely safe. All Cron parsing and translation happens locally in your browser. No data leaves your device.

Does it support Chinese output?

Yes. The tool outputs descriptions in Chinese when the Chinese interface is selected, and English when the English interface is selected.

What is the difference from Cron Parser?

Cron Parser shows specific next execution times; Cron to Text translates the meaning of the expression into natural language. They complement each other.
